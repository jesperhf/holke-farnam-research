Celestica is 4.5% of my portfolio at an average cost of $344.73, and it is the holding my [late-cycle entry](the-ai-trade-in-a-late-cycle.html) said to cut first if the AI capex cycle turns. It reported its third quarter after the close on October 26 and gave its first outlook for 2027. This is the update to the valuation behind my positions page.

## Summary

**The quarter changed the earnings, not the value.** Celestica now expects $40 billion of revenue in 2027, against about $35 billion that analysts expected and $32 billion in my base case. My base value still only moves from $202 to $199 a share, because the growth needs $1.5 to $2 billion of capex, a large build in working capital, and the 11 million shares sold in August.

| | Before results (Oct 23 model) | After results |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027 revenue in my base case | $32.0B | $37.0B |
| Bear value | $109 | $126 |
| Base value | $202 | $199 |
| Bull value | $306 | $317 |
| Base-case return at the price | 8.8% a year at $264.59 | 8.9% at $246.65; about 8.1% at $284.90 |
| Trim above (bull value) | $305 | $315 |
| Exit below (halfway, bear to base) | $155 | $160 |

- **The beat was broad.** Revenue grew 83% and adjusted EPS 109%, both above the top of guidance, and the adjusted operating margin hit a record 8.5%.
- **The bear case improved the most.** A $40 billion outlook and customer forecasts for 2028 above it make a sudden collapse less likely, so my bear value rises 16%.
- **The price already reflects most of it.** The stock was up about 15% before the open, at $284.90. At that price my base case earns about 8.1% a year, below the 10.5% I require for a business this cyclical.
- **The thesis-break signal is further away than ever.** I said I would sell if a major hyperscaler cut capex or if 2027 guidance came in below 2026. Guidance came in at nearly double 2026.

I am holding. I will not add above $200, and I will trim a third above $315.

## Why the value barely moved

**1. More shares, and a correction to my last model.** Celestica had 126.1 million shares and $3.1 billion of net cash at September 30. My October 23 model used 115 million shares and $0.4 billion of net debt, figures from before the August offering that my data source had not yet updated. Fixing that alone moves the old base value from $202 to $212. The new cash and the new shares mostly offset each other, which is what an offering near fair value should do.

**2. Earnings are not cash yet.** The 2027 outlook implies about $2.7 billion of adjusted earnings. Capex of $1.5 to $2 billion and the working capital needed to nearly double revenue absorb almost all of it. In my base case, free cash flow is slightly negative in 2027 and about $1.3 billion in 2028, or roughly $10 a share. At $285, that is a 3.6% free cash flow yield in the second year of the ramp.

**3. The value sits after 2028, and this report does not settle that.** 54% of the base value comes from the terminal value, the years after 2036. My base case lands 2027 about 8% below the outlook at $37 billion, grows to $42 billion in 2028, dips in 2029 as customers digest the build, and settles at a 7% operating margin after stock pay. The margins are after stock pay, about 0.4 points below the adjusted figure, as in the rest of my holdings models. Each of those choices matters less than the margin a contract manufacturer can hold once the build slows.

| Change to the base case | Base value |
| --- | --- |
| Base case as built | $199 |
| 2027 at the full $40B outlook, same shape after | $209 |
| No digestion year in 2029 | $211 |
| Adjusted margins, with no stock pay deducted | $213 |
| Long-run margin of 8% instead of 7% | $221 |

Even the most generous single change gets the base value to about $220. To justify $285 at a 10.5% discount rate, I need several of these together: the full outlook, no pause, and margins that hold near 8% for good. That is close to my bull case.

**4. Concentration cuts both ways.** Three customers buying two-thirds of the output is why the ramp is so steep, and it is why one of them changing its plans could undo it.

## What it means for the late-cycle view

This is the strongest evidence yet against my view that AI capex peaks in 2027. A supplier to the largest hyperscalers and AI labs says its customers' 2028 forecasts sit meaningfully above a 2027 plan that is already nearly double this year. The hyperscaler capex signal on my tracker has not fired, and this makes it less likely to fire soon. Two caveats keep me from changing the call. Customer forecasts are not orders, and a sector can grow into 2028 while its stocks are already priced for that. Both can be true: the cycle can run longer than I expected, and the stocks can still offer less than a full return.

## What I am doing

- **Hold.** The thesis-break signal is nowhere near firing, and the business is doing better than my old base case.
- **No adds above $200**, the new base value. At today's price the stock earns less than my required return unless the bull case plays out.
- **Trim a third above $315**, the new bull value, up from $305. My cost is $344.73, so a trim there would still be at a loss.
- **Review below $160**, halfway between the new bear and base values.
- **Tax.** The position is below cost. Any sale this year realizes a loss that can offset the gain on the ServiceNow trim from October 26. I am not a tax adviser, and I will decide on that before year-end, not today.
