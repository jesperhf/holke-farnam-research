## Executive summary

**The macro cycle is late, but the AI capex cycle is not finished; the best upside sits with the companies that own physical bottlenecks and do not need cheap debt.**

- **Business cycle: late expansion with a stagflationary tilt.** The Fed hiked to 3.75% to 4.00% in September, the first hike since 2023, as an oil shock lifted CPI to 3.7%. Payrolls slowed to +29k, and the 10-year sits above 5.3%.
- **AI cycle: entering its debt-funded phase.** Demand is real (Nvidia revenue up 106%, frontier lab run rates near $65B to $70B) and 2027 hyperscaler capex is tracking toward about $1 trillion. But AI-related debt issuance is on pace for about $570B this year, and vendor financing is appearing. Historically this phase precedes the peak in supplier stocks by months, not weeks.
- **The variable to watch is the cost of capital, not AI demand.** The 1999 to 2000 analog fits: the Fed hiked into a tech capex boom with oil rising, and the build broke where financing was weakest.
- **Highest upside on a risk-adjusted basis:** power and thermal equipment, semiconductor equipment, and networking and optics. Memory and custom silicon have strong momentum but higher timing risk. Neoclouds, levered developers and pre-revenue physical AI screen worst.

## Where the business cycle stands

The US is in a late-cycle expansion with a stagflationary tilt: growth is positive but slowing, an energy supply shock is pushing headline inflation up, and the Fed has restarted hiking. Corporate earnings remain the one indicator still behaving like mid-cycle, and most of that strength is AI and energy.

**What this means.** Three classic late-cycle markers are present at once: a central bank tightening, a commodity spike, and a long end of the curve repricing higher. The labor market is the swing factor; a September payroll print of 29k with downward revisions makes further hikes less certain, and futures put the odds of an October hold at about 77% after the report ([BabyPips](https://www.babypips.com/news/headline-us-jobs-report-september-2026-payrolls-miss-fed-hike-odds)).

**The closest analog is 1999 to 2000.** Then, as now, the Fed hiked into a technology capex boom while oil roughly doubled and the long end rose. The boom did not end because demand for the technology vanished; it ended when the cost of capital rose enough to break the financing of the marginal builder. That is the lens I apply to the AI trade.

**My base case (next 6 to 12 months):** slow growth with sticky headline inflation, one or two more Fed hikes, and a 10-year yield that stays near 5%. Recession is a real risk in 2027 if energy prices stay elevated, but it is not the base case while capex and earnings remain this strong.

## Where the AI trade sits within the cycle

The AI buildout runs on its own cycle, and it is further from its peak than the macro cycle is. But it has entered the phase where debt, not free cash flow, funds the marginal dollar of capex. That is the phase that ended the 1990s telecom build, so I treat the cost of capital, not AI demand, as the variable most likely to end the trade.


**Demand is real and still accelerating.** This is the key difference from 2000, when much of the fiber laid sat unused. Nvidia reported $96.2B of revenue for the quarter ended July 26, up 106% year over year, and guided the October quarter to $108B ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm)); its CFO gave a preliminary view of roughly 70% growth for fiscal 2028, which is mostly calendar 2027 ([XTB](https://www.xtb.com/ae-en/market-analysis/news-and-research/nvidia-shares-react-to-solid-earnings-report-and-stonger-guidance-what-s-next-for-the-ai-giant)). On the end-demand side, OpenAI's run rate is nearing $70B and Anthropic's was about $65B in July ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/09/29/scoop-openais-annual-recurring-revenue-nears-70b)).

**Capex is still being revised up.** After the July earnings round, 2026 guidance stood at about $175B for Microsoft (calendar 2026), about $220B for Amazon, $195B to $205B for Alphabet, $130B to $145B for Meta, and about $70B net for Oracle's fiscal 2027 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/03/microsoft-and-amazon-won-the-ai-spending-week/)). Moody's sees hyperscaler capex closing in on $1 trillion in 2027 ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)).

**But the funding mix has shifted to debt.** Morgan Stanley expects about $570B of AI-related debt issuance in 2026, with $236B raised by May 31 ([Allwork, citing Morgan Stanley](https://allwork.space/2026/06/morgan-stanley-forecasts-570-billion-ai-debt-wave-as-hyperscalers-race-to-fund-growth/)). Investment-grade AI bonds reached $218B by July 8, versus $80.5B in all of 2025 ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/bonds/bond-issuance-backing-ai-investment-tops-250b-testing-limits-voracious-investor-demand)). In a Bank of America survey, 48% of fund managers named hyperscaler capex the most likely source of a systemic credit event ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/ai-data-center-debt-has-climbed-to-the-top-of-wall-streets-credit-risk-watchlist/)). Vendor financing is creeping in too: Nvidia is now helping finance whole data center projects ([XTB](https://www.xtb.com/ae-en/market-analysis/news-and-research/nvidia-shares-react-to-solid-earnings-report-and-stonger-guidance-what-s-next-for-the-ai-giant)).

**Physical bottlenecks extend the cycle.** Micron says it has no line of sight to memory supply and demand rebalancing, with markets tighter in the next two years than in 2026 ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/micron-record-quarter-memory-shortage-2028)). A heavy-duty gas turbine ordered today from GE Vernova arrives around 2031 ([OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Gas-Turbine-Shortage-Just-Became-AIs-Biggest-Constraint.amp.html)). Scarcity cuts both ways: it caps how fast anyone can overbuild, and it hands pricing power to whoever owns the bottleneck.

**The market is already discriminating.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had its best start to a year on record, up 81% through May ([Bespoke](https://www.bespokepremium.com/morning-lineup/bespokes-morning-lineup-6-1-26-the-year-of-semis/)), then gave back ground and stood up 48% on a total-return basis by July 29 ([First Trust](https://www.ftportfolios.com/blogs/InvestBlog/2026/7/30/factoid---thursday,-july-30,-2026)). Meanwhile software ETF IGV fell about 27% in the SaaS selloff ([Schwab Network](https://schwabnetwork.com/articles/ai-disruption-continues-not-just-software)), and Oracle, the most levered spender, fell about 35% after its June earnings report ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/03/microsoft-and-amazon-won-the-ai-spending-week/)).

**My read:** capex should keep growing through 2027 because it is supply-constrained and contracted, but the growth rate likely peaks in 2027. In past capex booms, the stocks of suppliers peaked months before spending itself peaked, on the second derivative. That argues for owning bottlenecks with contracted pricing, avoiding businesses that need cheap debt to survive, and starting to watch for the turn in growth rates rather than levels.

## The AI value chain

The AI trade is not one sector but a stack of eleven, and the money moves downward: application and cloud revenue funds capex, which becomes orders for chips, which become orders for tools, power and cooling.


The lower a layer sits, the longer its lead times and the later its revenue arrives in the cycle, which is why I favor the bottom of the stack at this stage.

## Sector-by-sector assessment

Eleven sub-sectors make up the investable AI trade. Each is judged on the same four questions: how hard is its bottleneck, how visible is its revenue, how much does it depend on cheap capital, and how much good news is already priced in. Example companies are listed for orientation only, not as recommendations.

### 1. Power and thermal equipment

*Vertiv, Eaton, GE Vernova, Schneider Electric, Siemens Energy*

- **Thesis:** power is now the binding constraint on AI capacity, and equipment lead times run years past the GPU cycle. GE Vernova is selling gas turbine slots for 2031 delivery ([OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Gas-Turbine-Shortage-Just-Became-AIs-Biggest-Constraint.amp.html)) and reported a $176B backlog with Q2 gas orders up fourfold ([SentiSense](https://app.sentisense.ai/stories/ai-power-crunch-boosts-ge-vernova-as-energy-stocks-gain-spotlight-09022026)).
- **Evidence:** Eaton's data center orders rose 85% in Q2 ([Crypto Briefing, citing Morgan Stanley](https://cryptobriefing.com/morgan-stanley-ai-data-centers-38-gigawatt-power-gap/)); Vertiv grew Q2 sales 24% and raised 2026 guidance ([Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/financial-news/show/3267999-vertiv-data-center-stock-gains/)). Liquid cooling is now standard in Nvidia's reference designs.
- **Risks:** premium multiples (Vertiv traded near 53x forward earnings in midsummer, per [Gotrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/blog/vertiv-vrt-data-center-cooling-ai-2026/)); slot reservations can lapse if data centers are cancelled; Vertiv stopped disclosing orders and backlog.
- **Cycle fit:** strong. Backlogs convert to revenue through 2028 even if capex growth slows, and grid and reshoring demand provide a second customer base.
- **Upside view: High.**

### 2. Semiconductor equipment and foundry

*ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, TSMC*

- **Thesis:** every other bottleneck (memory, logic, packaging) is solved by buying more tools, and tools are ordered years ahead. TSMC's equipment needs reached about 1.9x its own December 2025 forecast and it lifted 2026 capex to $60B to $64B ([HWBusters](https://hwbusters.com/?p=113497)).
- **Evidence:** Creative Strategies now puts 2026 wafer fab equipment at $153B, above its prior bull case ([The Diligence Stack](https://www.thediligencestack.com/p/chip-equipment-just-beat-our-bull)); ASML says some customers are discussing orders for 2029 and 2030 ([Arise](https://www.arise.tv/tsmc-signals-caution-over-2027-outlook-despite-record-ai-driven-profits/)). HBM uses three to four times the wafer capacity of standard DRAM, which multiplies tool demand.
- **Risks:** TSMC is cautious on 2027; China export controls; equipment is the classic late-cycle order book that gets cut first when memory makers pause.
- **Cycle fit:** good for the next 12 to 18 months; it lags chip demand, so it peaks later than chips do.
- **Upside view: High.**

### 3. Networking and optics

*Arista, Broadcom (switching), Lumentum, Coherent, Credo, Corning, Fabrinet, Ciena*

- **Thesis:** as clusters scale past what copper can connect, optics moves from a component to a platform. Goldman Sachs sizes AI optical networking at about $15B in 2026 rising to $154B in 2028, with co-packaged optics potentially $91B of that ([LinkedIn summary of Goldman](https://tw.linkedin.com/in/andrew-men-4510b613)).
- **Evidence:** Lumentum's optical circuit switch backlog passed $400M and it took a multi-hundred-million order for co-packaged optics lasers ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/lumentum-and-coherent-nasdaq-stocks-climb-following-bullish-analyst-coverage-on-ai-networking-demand)). Broadcom's AI networking revenue rose more than 2.5x year over year ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/broadcom-q3-earnings-call-highlights)).
- **Risks:** stocks have run hard (Lumentum up 158% year to date per TIKR); high customer concentration (84% of Credo's revenue from four customers); substrate and laser supply limits; the timing of the co-packaged optics ramp could slip to 2028.
- **Cycle fit:** good. The biggest step-up (scale-up optics) lands in 2027 to 2028, after today's compute ramp.
- **Upside view: High, with high volatility.**

### 4. Memory and storage

*Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung, plus HDD and NAND makers*

- **Thesis:** HBM absorbs wafer capacity, so conventional DRAM and NAND are short as well. Micron's quarter to September 3 brought in $54.2B of revenue, nearly five times a year earlier, with guidance of $61.5B and about 75% of 2027 output already committed ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/micron-record-quarter-memory-shortage-2028)).
- **Evidence:** SK Hynix expects 2027 to be the tightest year ([Mezha, citing Reuters](https://mezha.ua/en/news/ceo-sk-hynix-2027-rik-bude-naygirshim-v-plani-deficitu-dram-313157/)); TrendForce sees HBM contract prices up more than 50% in 2027 ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/hbm-dram-ai-memory-demand)).
- **Risks:** memory is the most cyclical corner of tech, and memory stocks historically peak when the rate of price increase peaks, not when prices peak. Micron is already up about 213% this year (Mezha). An antitrust suit alleges supply coordination, and Chinese supplier CXMT is gaining share.
- **Cycle fit:** earnings momentum is the strongest in the AI complex, but this is the latest-cycle position on the board.
- **Upside view: Medium-High for 6 to 12 months, falling after that.**

### 5. Custom silicon (ASICs)

*Broadcom, Marvell, Alchip, Astera Labs*

- **Thesis:** large buyers are designing their own accelerators to cut cost per token. Broadcom's AI revenue reached $16.7B in its July quarter, up 221%, and management guided AI revenue to roughly $115B in fiscal 2027 and $230B in fiscal 2028 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/broadcom-q3-earnings-call-highlights)).
- **Evidence:** contracted programs with Google (TPU), Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta, with supply secured for fiscal 2027 per management.
- **Risks:** gross margin is falling as memory content rises; a handful of customers; the stock fell on a Q4 guide that missed the highest expectations ([Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/articles/614716/broadcom-stock-slipped-while-its-revenue-schedule-got-longer/2026-09-09)).
- **Cycle fit:** the best revenue visibility in semis, multi-year contracts.
- **Upside view: Medium-High.**

### 6. Merchant accelerators (GPUs)

*Nvidia, AMD*

- **Thesis:** still the center of the build. Nvidia guided its October quarter to $108B and Vera Rubin is in full production ([Rivkin](https://rivkin.com.au/?p=37534)).
- **Evidence:** the CFO's preliminary view of about 70% growth in fiscal 2028 was well above the roughly 44% investors expected ([XTB](https://www.xtb.com/ae-en/market-analysis/news-and-research/nvidia-shares-react-to-solid-earnings-report-and-stonger-guidance-what-s-next-for-the-ai-giant)).
- **Risks:** at about $5.5T of market value, the upside per dollar is lower; gross margin is guided to bottom at 71% to 72% (from 75%); share is shifting toward custom chips; Nvidia now helps finance customer data centers, a late-cycle signal. Nvidia has lagged the rest of the semiconductor index for about a year ([Bespoke](https://www.bespokepremium.com/morning-lineup/bespokes-morning-lineup-6-1-26-the-year-of-semis/)).
- **Cycle fit:** neutral. Quality is unmatched, but it is a consensus position.
- **Upside view: Medium.**

### 7. Power generation and nuclear

*Constellation, Vistra, Talen, NRG, gas-fired independent power producers*

- **Thesis:** existing firm generation is scarce and being locked up on long contracts. Constellation signed 3,590 MW with Google and will invest more than $4.3B in uprates; PJM capacity prices are up more than elevenfold since 2024 ([Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/10/06/md-based-constellation-power-deal-google/)). Vistra signed 20-year PPAs with Meta for 2,609 MW ([Vistra 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001692819/000169281926000019/vistra-20260630.htm)).
- **Risks:** political pushback on power bills (PJM governors seek price collars; FERC scrutiny of co-location, per [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/18/crowd-dumping-vistra-why-buy-stock-down/)); high rates hurt utility-like valuations.
- **Cycle fit:** contracted, inflation-linked cash flows suit a late cycle, though regulation caps the upside.
- **Upside view: Medium.**

### 8. Hyperscalers and cloud platforms

*Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle*

- **Thesis:** the owners of both the compute and the customers. Cloud growth across AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud in Q1 was the fastest since 2021, and about $700B was added to backlogs over two quarters ([DCD, citing Moody's](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)).
- **Risks:** capex is eating operating cash flow while debt and leases rise (same source); investors now punish the heaviest spenders, as with Meta and Oracle after July.
- **Cycle fit:** the cash-rich names (Microsoft, Alphabet) are the defensive way to own AI; Oracle is the levered way.
- **Upside view: Medium-Low near term**, but the most resilient in a downturn.

### 9. Neoclouds and data center developers

*CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN, Equinix, Digital Realty*

- **Thesis:** GPU rental prices are rising even for older chips; Nebius is lifting H100 on-demand pricing 17% from October 1 ([I/O Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/nebius-vs-coreweave-ai-stock)).
- **Risks:** this is where leverage lives. CoreWeave carries about $35B of debt against $2.3B of cash ([Barchart via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15282437098382336)), and Morgan Stanley projects its debt reaching $92B by 2028 ([LongPort](https://longportapp.cn/news/282952306)). With the 10-year above 5%, refinancing risk is the central issue.
- **Cycle fit:** poor. Highest beta to both GPU pricing and credit spreads; the 2000 analog is the competitive local exchange carriers.
- **Upside view: Low on a risk-adjusted basis.** Nebius's balance sheet is materially stronger than CoreWeave's.

### 10. Software and AI applications

*Salesforce, ServiceNow, Adobe, Palantir, Snowflake, Datadog; pre-IPO OpenAI and Anthropic*

- **Thesis (contrarian):** the SaaS selloff priced in wholesale displacement by AI agents; Adobe and Salesforce traded at roughly half of early 2025 levels ([Betterment](https://www.betterment.com/advisors/resources/ai-software-stocks-market-news-may-2026)). If incumbents prove they can charge for agents, this is the cheapest part of the AI trade.
- **Risks:** seat-based pricing is genuinely threatened; the burden of proof is on the companies; the pending OpenAI and Anthropic IPOs could pull capital away from incumbents.
- **Cycle fit:** this is the monetization stage of the AI cycle, which comes later. Valuation support is better than anywhere else in AI.
- **Upside view: Medium, high dispersion.** A stock picker's sector.

### 11. Physical AI and edge devices

*Tesla (Optimus), robotics supply chain, Qualcomm, Apple, PC makers*

- **Thesis:** robotics is the next platform, but leaders are mostly private (Figure, Agility, Unitree's pending IPO) and commercial volume is minimal; prediction markets gave Optimus only about a 14% chance of a 2026 commercial release ([AI2.Work](https://ai2.work/blog/the-physical-ai-boom-inside-the-humanoid-robot-race)).
- **Risks:** memory costs are a direct headwind for devices; Moody's expects PC and smartphone units to fall by double digits in 2026 ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/moodys-hyperscaler-capex-forecasts-marked-up-by-85bn-to-close-in-on-1trn-by-2027/)).
- **Cycle fit:** long-dated optionality; the least suited to a late-cycle, high-rate tape.
- **Upside view: Low near term.**

## Key risks and signposts

The trade most likely ends through the cost of capital, not through a collapse in AI demand. These are the indicators that would tell me the phase is changing, in rough order of importance.

| Signpost | What would worry me | Where it stands now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10-yr Treasury and real yield | 10-yr sustained above 5.5% or real yield above 3% | 5.31% nominal, 2.95% real (Oct 6) |
| Fed path | Hikes continuing into 2027 while payrolls weaken | Hiked to 3.75% to 4.00% in Sept; October hold odds about 77% |
| AI credit markets | Failed or repriced data center deals; widening hyperscaler CDS | Signs of fatigue: some buyers passed on a $14B Oracle data center bond and Meta CDS hit records ([Tiger Brokers](https://www.itiger.com/news/1151480590)) |
| 2027 capex guidance | Any hyperscaler guiding 2027 growth sharply below 2026 | Q3 reports due in late October; Alphabet already flagged a significant 2027 increase |
| Vendor financing and working capital | Suppliers funding customers; inventories and receivables rising faster than sales | Nvidia inventory rose to $32B and DSO to 60 days ([call summary](https://transcripts.platformaeronaut.com/summaries/NVDA-2Q27-AI-Summary)) |
| Memory pricing momentum | Quarterly contract price gains decelerating | DRAM contracts up 15% to 18% q/q in Q3 (Mezha, citing TrendForce) |
| GPU rental prices | Falling rates for current-generation GPUs | Rising, even for H100s |
| AI lab revenue and IPOs | Run-rate growth stalling, or IPO pricing weak | OpenAI near $70B run rate; both labs preparing IPOs |
| Energy shock | Brent staying above $110 into 2027, forcing more hikes | Brent near $114 in September |
| Power regulation | PJM price collars, FERC limits on co-location | Under active discussion |

**Other risks that cut across all sectors:**

- **Concentration:** a handful of buyers (four hyperscalers, two frontier labs) drive most of the demand, so one buyer's pause ripples through every supplier.
- **Geopolitics:** Nvidia guides with zero China data center revenue; Taiwan concentration in leading-edge manufacturing remains the tail risk for the whole stack.
- **Circularity:** labs, clouds and chipmakers increasingly invest in, lend to and buy from one another, which can overstate end demand.

**Near-term catalysts:** Vertiv reports October 22; the hyperscalers report in late October; the FOMC meets in late October.

## What I chose to research next

I took **power and thermal equipment** and **semiconductor equipment and foundry** to company-level research first, with **networking and optics** as a higher-volatility third. I added **memory and storage** as a fourth, because I wanted to test whether memory pricing power could be the next big cyclical pricing story. All three own physical bottlenecks with multi-year backlogs, and none depends on cheap debt to survive, which is the profile that holds up best in a late-cycle, rising-rate tape.

**How I framed the sectors at the time:**

- **Core (bottleneck owners):** power and thermal equipment, semiconductor equipment. Longest visibility, latest in the capex chain, least exposed to GPU pricing.
- **Satellite (higher beta):** networking and optics; custom silicon. Strongest new-product cycles, but crowded and concentrated.
- **Tactical (time-limited):** memory. Only worth owning with a clear signal for when contract-price growth is slowing.
- **Contrarian option:** AI-exposed software, where valuations already discount heavy disruption.
- **Avoid or underweight:** neoclouds and levered data center developers, and pre-revenue physical AI.

For each of the four, the next step was to screen the listed companies on backlog coverage, pricing power, balance sheet, customer concentration and valuation against growth, then value the strongest names one by one. Those company work-ups are the next three entries: [memory and storage](memory-and-storage.html), [power and chip equipment](power-and-chip-equipment.html) and [optical networking](optical-networking.html).
