## Why optics, and how each one gets paid

**AI clusters are outgrowing copper, so the links between chips are moving to light.** Goldman Sachs sizes AI optical networking at about $15B in 2026 and $154B in 2028, with co-packaged optics, where the optical engine sits inside the chip package, potentially $91B of that ([LinkedIn summary of Goldman research](https://tw.linkedin.com/in/andrew-men-4510b613)). The two companies capture that spend in very different ways.

|  | Coherent | Fabrinet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it sells | Its own lasers (indium phosphide), transceivers and optical circuit switches | Contract manufacturing of optical modules and systems that customers design |
| Who owns the design | Coherent | The customer (Cisco, Nvidia, Nokia, Amazon) |
| How it earns | Product margins: 40% gross, about 22% operating (non-GAAP) | A manufacturing fee: about 12% gross, 11% operating |
| What co-packaged optics does to it | New laser and engine demand; revenue starts in the December quarter | Neutral to positive if it keeps assembling the new modules; it is working on them with several customers |
| Biggest risk | Capacity spending runs ahead of demand | Losing a top customer's program |

Co-packaged optics is a risk for every maker of plug-in transceivers, because it moves part of the value into the chip package, where TSMC, Broadcom and Nvidia sit. Coherent is better placed than most because it makes the lasers that co-packaged designs still need.

## Coherent (COHR)

**Verdict: pass. My base case is worth $112, 52% below the $234.86 price, and today's price earns about 7.9% a year if that case plays out.** Growth is real, but it is consuming cash, and the stock prices the $3B-a-quarter target as already achieved.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (April to June) revenue was $2.05B, up 42% pro forma, with data center revenue up 66%, a 40.2% gross margin and $1.74 of non-GAAP EPS. Fiscal Q1 2027 guidance is $2.2B to $2.4B. Management targets over $3B of quarterly revenue by the end of fiscal 2027 and expects co-packaged optics revenue to start this quarter. Indium phosphide laser output is set to double year over year, and debt leverage fell to 0.7 times ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/coherents-q4-earnings-call-set-a-new-target-3-billion-in-quarterly-revenue-heres-what-that-means-for-the-stock)).

**Why it fails the durability test.** Free cash flow was negative $1.0B in fiscal 2026 as quarterly capex jumped to $556M from $131M a year earlier. Revenue fell 9% in fiscal 2024 and the operating margin dropped to 2.6% ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cohr/financials/)). It carries $1.6B of net debt and a 2.11 beta. A company spending ahead of demand at that scale is exactly what a 2027 to 2028 slowdown punishes.

| Scenario | Revenue, FY27 to FY31 ($B) | Operating margin path | Terminal margin | Value per share | vs $234.86 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear: AI optics orders fall in FY28 | 9.2, 8.0, 8.3, 9.0, 9.8 | 18% falling to 12%, then 15% | 14% | $38 | −84% |
| Base: near the $3B quarterly target, then slower | 10.2, 12.5, 13.0, 14.5, 16.0 | 20% to 22% | 19% | $112 | −52% |
| Bull: target beaten, co-packaged optics ramps | 11.0, 14.5, 17.0, 19.5, 22.0 | 21% rising to 24% | 21% | $182 | −23% |

Margins are about 2 points below non-GAAP to reflect amortization and stock pay. Net reinvestment starts at 12% of revenue in fiscal 2027 for the capacity build and falls to 4%. Discount rate 11.5%; on the calculated rate (13.4%) the base value is $85.

**Entry range.** Below about $110. Revisit if free cash flow turns positive for two straight quarters while growth holds above 30%. Next report: November 4, after the close.

## Fabrinet (FN)

**Verdict: a watchlist name close to fair value. My base case is worth $279, 21% below the $355.48 price, and today's price earns about 8.7% a year if that case plays out.** Of the two optics names, this is the one that passes the durability test.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (April to June) revenue was $1.32B, up 45%, with $4.10 of non-GAAP EPS. Fiscal Q1 2027 guidance is $1.375B to $1.425B of revenue and $4.10 to $4.25 of non-GAAP EPS ([Fabrinet 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001408710/000140871026000026/fn-2026811xex991q426.htm)). Data center revenue was 51% of sales and grew 68%. Building 10 in Thailand opens in early 2027 and adds roughly $3B to $3.5B of capacity, against an exit run rate of about $5.3B ([Nasdaq call summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/fabrinet-q4-earnings-call-highlights)).

**Why it passes.** Revenue grew every year from fiscal 2022 to 2026 and the operating margin stayed between 9% and 10% throughout ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fn/financials/)). It has $871M of net cash, essentially no debt and a 1.22 beta. As the manufacturer rather than the designer, it gets paid whichever customer's product wins.

**What to watch.** Four customers were each at least 10% of fiscal 2026 revenue: Cisco 20%, Nvidia 16%, Nokia 11% and Amazon 11%. Free cash flow was only $4M for the year because inventory and capacity spending absorbed the profit. The stock fell 23% in the week of its August report on valuation and margin worries, not a broken quarter ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/why-fabrinet-shed-nearly-quarter-its-value-week)).

| Scenario | Revenue, FY27 to FY31 ($B) | Operating margin path | Terminal margin | Value per share | vs $355.48 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear: a top customer program lost plus AI slowdown | 5.6, 4.8, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0 | 10% falling to 8.5%, then 9.5% | 9.0% | $169 | −52% |
| Base: Building 10 fills, growth slows after FY28 | 6.2, 7.8, 8.2, 9.2, 10.2 | 10.0% to 10.5% | 9.5% | $279 | −21% |
| Bull: capacity fills toward $12B+ | 6.6, 9.0, 11.0, 12.5, 13.5 | 10.8% rising to 11.0% | 10.5% | $388 | +9% |

Values use 36.3M diluted shares. Price is the October 20 close. Discount rate 10%; on the calculated rate (11.1%) the base value is $238.

**Entry range.** Below about $280. Next report: November 2, after the close; watch for new customer programs and whether free cash flow recovers.

## How they compare with the rest of the shortlist

**Fabrinet lands fourth of the eight long-term names, just behind TSMC, nVent and Vertiv; Coherent sits near the bottom with the chip equipment makers.** No name clears its required return in the base case, which repeats the finding of the last pass. The [late-cycle synthesis](the-ai-trade-in-a-late-cycle.html) charts all twelve names against their base values.


For a 3 to 5 year portfolio, the shortlist now narrows to three names worth buying on a pullback of about 20%: TSMC, nVent and Fabrinet. Each has net cash or modest debt, margins that held through the last downturn, and a buy-below price within roughly a fifth of today's level.
