Second company work-up after the [sector map](the-ai-trade-and-the-business-cycle.html), covering the six core names in power and chip equipment. After [memory](memory-and-storage.html) turned out to be a set of swing trades, I judged these as 3 to 5 year holds that must survive an AI capex slowdown in 2027 to 2028, not as swing trades.

## Summary

**My suspicion going in was that none of these six would offer an adequate return at today's price, even as a long-term hold. The numbers agree.** If my base cases play out, today's prices earn 7.4% to 9.5% a year, only 2 to 4 points above a 5.31% 10-year Treasury. The issue is the price the whole market is paying for AI infrastructure, not any single company.

| Rank for a 3 to 5 year hold | Company | Price (Oct 21) | Base-case return from today | Buy below |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | TSMC (ADR) | $426.65 | 9.5% a year (10.4% via Taiwan shares) | $345 |
| 2 | nVent | $139.78 | 8.8% | $110 |
| 3 | ASML | $1,677.89 | 7.7% | $1,085 |
| 4 | Vertiv | $211.54 | 8.8% | $145 |
| 5 | Siemens Energy | €133.00 | 7.7% | €80 |
| 6 | Applied Materials | $410.41 | 7.4% | $230 |

The ranking weighs durability as well as return. ASML ranks above Vertiv despite a lower return because it came through 2009 with its monopoly intact; Vertiv's margin collapsed as recently as 2022.

- **TSMC is the one to own first.** It needs about a 19% pullback, has $100B of net cash, and shrank only 5% in the 2023 downturn.
- **nVent is the steadiest business.** Its margins held between 15% and 18% for five years, and about 60% of 2026 sales come from grid, industrial and building work outside data centers.
- **ASML is the best franchise, but needs a 35% drop.** That has happened before: sales fell 46% in 2009.
- **The macro is the binding constraint.** Each point of extra discount rate cuts values 10% to 15%, and Treasuries pay 5.3% while I wait.

The full model, with a switch between a calculated and an assumed WACC, is on the [models page](../models.html). Coherent and Fabrinet, the two satellite names, are in the [next entry](optical-networking.html).

## The durability test

**A 3 to 5 year hold changes the question from "what happens next quarter" to "what does this company look like after a downturn, and did I pay too much going in".** Each name is tested on three things: how badly it fell in past downturns, whether its balance sheet and backlog can carry it through one, and what return today's price offers if my base case comes true.

| Company | Worst past downturn on record | Balance sheet today | What cushions a slowdown | 5-yr beta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TSMC | Revenue −11% in 2009 ([TSMC](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/1596)); −5% in 2023 | $100B net cash | Sole supplier of leading-edge AI chips; 79% of wafer revenue at 7nm or better | 1.24 |
| ASML | Sales −46% in 2009, with a net loss ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2010-01-asml-mln-euro-net-loss.html)) | $12B net cash | EUV monopoly; service and upgrades on the installed base are €3.3B a quarter; 2027 to 2028 demand exceeds planned supply | 1.30 |
| Applied Materials | Backlog −44% in fiscal 2009 ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000006951/000095012309070205/f53510e10vk.htm)); grew through 2023 | $1.9B net cash | Services are 20% of revenue, growing over 20%; China 26% of sales is a two-sided risk | 1.59 |
| Vertiv | Operating margin fell to 4% and free cash flow turned negative in 2022, even as revenue grew | $0.2B net debt | Backlog conversion; liquid cooling now standard in AI racks | 2.05 |
| Siemens Energy | Operating margin −11% in fiscal 2023 on wind turbine failures | €8.1B net cash | €162B backlog, about 3.7 years of revenue | 1.85 |
| nVent | Operating margin held between 15% and 18% from 2021 to 2025 | $1.4B net debt | $2.5B backlog; diversified electrical business beyond data centers | 1.34 |

Margins and 2022 to 2023 figures are from [Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com) annual statements.

**What this says.** TSMC and ASML are the only two that have come through a deep downturn with their franchise intact and cash to spare, and both have customers booking years ahead. Vertiv and Siemens Energy carry the most operating risk: each has had a margin collapse within the last four years, and both have the highest betas.

**How the models differ from the memory pass.** These are 10-year DCFs: five explicit years, then five years in which growth, margins and reinvestment fade toward terminal levels. That fits companies expected to compound rather than cycle. The bear case in every model is an AI capex slowdown in 2027 to 2028, sized to the company's own history. Each model can run on an assumed discount rate (10% to 11%) or a calculated one (11% to 13.5%, from CAPM with today's 5.31% Treasury yield).

## TSMC (TSM)

**Verdict: the best long-term holding in the group and the closest to fair value. My base case is worth $344 per ADR, 19% below the $426.65 price, and buying at today's price earns about 9.5% a year if that case plays out.** It is the one name I would buy on a modest pullback.

**Latest results.** Q3 2026 (July to September) revenue was $46.7B, up 41% in dollars, with a 66.4% gross margin and 58.7% operating margin. Q4 guidance is $52.3B to $54.3B at a 65% to 67% gross margin ([TSMC 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000726/a3q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)). 2026 capex was raised to $60B to $64B ([HWBusters](https://hwbusters.com/?p=113497)), while management has struck a cautious tone on 2027.

**Why it holds up over 3 to 5 years.** 79% of wafer revenue is at 7nm or better, where TSMC has no real competitor, and it holds $100B of net cash. In the 2023 chip downturn revenue fell only 5% and the operating margin stayed above 42%.

**One cost to avoid.** The ADR trades at about a 15% premium to the Taiwan-listed shares (the local price works out to about $372 per ADR). At the local price, the base-case return rises from 9.5% to about 10.4% a year, so the Taiwan shares are the better way to own it where a broker offers them.

| Base: AI growth continues, slower after 2028 | 230, 280, 320, 355, 390 | 58% easing to 53% | 50% | $344 | −19% |
| Bull: AI compounding through 2031 | 240, 310, 390, 460, 520 | 59% to 60%, then 57% | 53% | $494 | +16% |

Revenue starts from about $176B in 2026 (three reported quarters plus the Q4 guidance midpoint). Discount rate 11%, including 1 point for Taiwan risk; on the calculated rate (12.0%) the base value is $303.

**Entry range.** Below about $345 per ADR, where the base case earns my 11% required return. Watch the Q4 report in January for the 2027 capex and growth outlook.

## ASML (ASML)

**Verdict: the strongest franchise here, priced for it. My base case is worth $1,085, 35% below the $1,677.89 price, and today's price earns about 7.7% a year if that case plays out.** Own it after a drawdown, not now.

**Latest results.** Q3 2026 (July to September) sales were a record €11.7B at a 57.5% gross margin, with €3.3B from service and upgrades on the installed base. Q4 guidance is €14.2B to €15.2B, and the 2026 outlook is €44B to €45B at about 56% gross margin ([ASML 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000937966/000162828026065813/pressreleasefinancialresul.htm)). ASML bought back about €4.1B of stock in the quarter.

**Why it holds up over 3 to 5 years.** It is the only EUV supplier. Management says planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not meet demand even after a 30% capacity increase in 2027, and some customers want to place orders for 2029 and 2030. The installed-base business keeps earning when new tool orders stall.

**Why the price is still too high.** In 2009 sales fell 46% and ASML lost money. A smaller version of that in 2028 is my bear case, and today's price leaves no room for it.

**Entry range.** Below about $1,085. Next report: January.

## Applied Materials (AMAT)

**Verdict: pass for now. My base case is worth $227, 45% below the $410.41 price, and today's price earns about 7.4% a year if that case plays out, the lowest in the group.** Even my bull case ($293) is below the price.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q3 2026 (May to July) revenue was a record $9.1B, up 25%, at a 34% non-GAAP operating margin and $3.50 of EPS. Fiscal Q4 guidance is $10.25B (up 51%) and $4.02 of EPS. The services business grew 22% to $1.8B, and China was 26% of revenue ([MarketBeat call summary](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/applied-materials-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-13/)). Management expects 2027 to be "another strong year" and is preparing to double quarterly system output by 2028.

**What worries me for a 3 to 5 year hold.** Doubling output capacity is what equipment makers do near the top of a cycle. Free cash flow has trailed earnings ($5.6B over the last 12 months on $30.8B of revenue) because inventory grows with output. China is a quarter of sales and a policy risk in both directions.

**Entry range.** Below about $230. Next report: November 12, after the close.

## Vertiv (VRT)

**Verdict: wait for the October 28 report. My base case is worth $145, 31% below the $211.54 price, and today's price earns about 8.8% a year if that case plays out.** Growth is excellent, but the stock carries the highest beta here (2.05) and the margin history most exposed to a slowdown.

**Latest results.** Q2 2026 net sales were $3.27B, up 24% (18% organic), at a 22.6% adjusted operating margin, with $925M of adjusted free cash flow and a net cash position at quarter end. Q3 guidance is $3.65B to $3.85B (34% to 36% organic growth) at a 24% to 25% adjusted margin. Full-year 2026 guidance is $13.8B to $14.2B of sales, $6.65 to $6.75 of adjusted EPS and $2.4B to $2.6B of free cash flow ([Vertiv 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001674101/000162828026050323/q22026exhibit991vrt07292026.htm)).

**What to weigh for a 3 to 5 year hold.** Liquid cooling is now the default in AI racks, which gives Vertiv years of demand. Against that, its operating margin fell to 4% in 2022 when supply chains broke, Q2 revenue slipped on project delays, and it has stopped disclosing orders and backlog, so the next turn will be harder to see coming.

## Siemens Energy (ENR)

**Verdict: pass for now. My base case is worth €80, 40% below the €133.00 price, and today's price earns about 7.7% a year if that case plays out.** Its record backlog is the best downside protection in the group, but the market already pays for margins I have not yet seen.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q3 2026 (April to June) orders were a record €17.9B (book-to-bill 1.57) and revenue €11.4B, up 19%, with profit before special items of €1.6B, a record 14.2% margin. Gas Services took €10B of orders at a 17.3% margin, and the wind unit, Siemens Gamesa, made its first profit since fiscal 2022. Backlog reached €162B, and management expects about €8B of free cash flow for the year ([GuruFocus call summary](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9006525/siemens-energy-ag-smegf-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-orders-and-margins-signal-strong-momentum)).

**What to weigh for a 3 to 5 year hold.** A backlog of about 3.7 years of revenue makes Siemens Energy far less exposed to a 2027 orders pause than Vertiv. The risk is the wind business: one quality failure took the group margin to −11% in fiscal 2023, and Gamesa's 2.7% margin is still thin.

**What to weigh for a 3 to 5 year hold.** Data centers are now a large share of sales, but the rest of the business is electrical enclosures and connections for utilities, industry and buildings, which grow with grid spending whatever happens to AI. Net leverage is 1.2 times.

## Valuation

**None of the six clears my required return in its base case today. TSMC comes closest at 9.5% a year against an 11% hurdle; the rest offer 7% to 9%.** That is a premium of only 2 to 4 points over a 10-year Treasury yielding 5.31%, and a bear case cuts it to 0 to 2 points.


**What this means.** The problem is not any one company. The market is pricing quality AI infrastructure at an equity risk premium well below the 4 to 5 points that would normally compensate for its cyclicality, while Treasuries pay 5.3% and 3-month bills about 4%. For a 3 to 5 year holder, that makes waiting for better prices a paid option rather than a cost.
