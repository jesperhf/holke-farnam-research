First company work-up after the [sector map](the-ai-trade-and-the-business-cycle.html), covering Micron, SK hynix, Seagate and Western Digital. I went in with a thesis of my own: that memory pricing power could be the next big cyclical pricing cycle, the way oil was. This entry tests it.

## Summary

**Memory pricing power is real through 2028, but three of these four stocks already price it in. SK hynix is the exception on paper, and I still passed on it.**

- **On "memory is the next oil":** the comparison holds for DRAM and HBM through 2028 (three suppliers, multi-year contracts, two to three years to add a fab). It breaks over a full cycle because memory supply is manufactured, its cost falls every year, and no cartel enforces discipline.
- **The stocks already trade on the rate of change.** DRAM contract gains peaked near +96% in Q1 2026 and are slowing toward +10% to +15%; all four stocks are 27% to 58% off their highs while prices still rise.
- **Seagate is a hard drive company, not an SSD one.** Its pricing power is strong today, but Toshiba doubling capacity for about $400M shows how cheaply new supply arrives. SSD pricing power sits with the NAND makers, where price gains are speeding up again.
- **Why I passed anyway.** Every one of these is really a 6 to 18 month trade on where memory prices go next. This late in the business cycle, I do not want positions whose outcome depends on timing a commodity price.

## Is memory the next oil?

**Partly. Memory has oil-like pricing power for the next 18 to 24 months, strongest in DRAM and HBM, but it lacks the three things that keep oil prices high for a decade.** Supply is manufactured rather than discovered, the cost of each bit keeps falling, and no cartel enforces discipline.

| Pricing power test | Oil (cartel era) | DRAM and HBM | NAND flash (SSDs) | Hard drives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who controls supply | A cartel that sets output | 3 makers hold about 90%; China's CXMT about 7.7% and growing ([GodisaGeek](https://godisageek.com/?p=310734)) | 5 to 6 makers, more fragmented | 3 makers: Seagate, WD, Toshiba |
| How fast new supply arrives | Years; limited by geology | 2 to 3 years per fab; Micron is building clean rooms for late 2028 | Like DRAM, plus faster bit growth from layer stacking | Fast and cheap: Toshiba plans to double capacity by end of fiscal 2027 for about $400M ([Parameter](https://parameter.io/toshibas-400m-hard-drive-investment-triggers-sharp-selloff-in-seagate-stx-and-western-digital/)) |
| Does the cost of supply fall? | No; the best fields deplete | Yes, with each process node | Yes, faster than DRAM | Yes, about 10% a year per TB (WD) |
| Contract cover | Term contracts plus spot | Micron: long-term agreements over 35% of revenue through 2030; over 75% of 2027 output committed | Mostly quarterly pricing | Seagate: nearline allocated into 2028, priced through 2027 |
| How buyers respond | Slowly | Already trimming: memory content per server growth has "moderated" (Micron) | Same as DRAM | Lengthening planning horizons to lock supply |
| Grade | Benchmark | Strong through 2028 | Strong near term, weaker structure | Strong now, most fragile structure |


**The stocks already trade on the rate of change, not the price level.** DRAM contract gains peaked at roughly +96% in Q1 2026, and all four stocks are now 27% to 58% below their 52-week highs even though prices are still rising. That is the pattern oil producers show near a price peak, and it is the signal I flagged in the [sector map](the-ai-trade-and-the-business-cycle.html).

**Where the thesis is strongest.** HBM is custom, takes months to qualify, and has three suppliers, so it behaves most like a scarce commodity. The catch is that long-term contracts now fix HBM prices, which caps the upside as well as the downside; SK hynix's record one-day drop in July came partly from that ([Tiger Brokers](https://www.itiger.com/news/1172332256)).

**Where it is weakest.** Hard drives. Capacity is cheap to add, and Toshiba doubling output is the equivalent of a cartel member breaking ranks. Seagate fell 9% to 11% on the announcement.

**On SSDs:** Seagate does not make NAND flash and its revenue is overwhelmingly hard drives, so it is not an SSD pricing play. SSD pricing power accrues to the NAND makers (Samsung, SK hynix through Solidigm, Micron, Sandisk, Kioxia). NAND is the one market where price gains are speeding up again, which makes Sandisk or Kioxia a candidate for a later pass.

## Micron (MU)

**Verdict: wait. At $909.96 the stock already prices margins that never go back to the cycle average; my base value is $672, 26% below today's price.** Micron is the best-capitalized US memory name, but even bull-case shortage assumptions reach only about $1,090.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (June to August 2026) revenue was $54.2B, up 379% from a year earlier, at an 87% gross margin and $33.42 of non-GAAP EPS. Guidance for fiscal Q1 2027 (September to November 2026) is $61.5B of revenue and $38.15 of EPS, with an 86.25% gross margin that management called the low point for the year ([MarketBeat call summary](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-micron-technology-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-09-30/)). Micron holds $73.5B of cash and investments against $5.2B of debt.

**Pricing power.** DRAM prices rose in the high teens and NAND about 30% in the quarter. Micron has 26 multi-year customer agreements it expects to make up over 35% of revenue through 2030, three-quarters of them with set pricing frameworks, about $150B of remaining obligations at set prices, and $32B of customer deposits. Over 75% of calendar 2027 output is committed. Management expects prices to keep rising after this quarter, but more slowly.

**The risk inside the good news.** First-half fiscal 2027 capex is about $25B and the second half will be higher, mostly to build clean rooms for late 2028. That is the supply that ends the shortage. Industry DRAM bit supply is already expected to grow in the low 20% range in 2027 and 2028.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear: AI capex slows in 2027, prices roll over late 2027 | 245, 150, 110, 130, 150 | 80% falling to 15%, then 33% | 25% | $351 | −61% |
| Base: tight through FY28, normal by FY30 | 260, 250, 200, 190, 205 | 82% falling to 45% | 35% | $672 | −26% |

**What the price implies.** To justify $910, Micron would need to earn about a 59% EBIT margin through the whole cycle, versus the 35% I assume, or keep growing revenue about 6% a year after fiscal 2027 with no downturn at all. At a 12% discount rate the base value is $672; at 11% it is $725 and at 13% it is $629.

**Entry range.** Below about $570 (base value less a 15% margin of safety). What would change the view: contract prices still rising in mid-2027, or capital returns large enough to retire a meaningful share of stock at today's price. Buybacks increase from December 9.

**Risks to the view, both ways:** a longer shortage (bull), a sharper AI capex pause (bear), US tariff or China policy changes, and the industry antitrust suit.

## SK hynix (000660)

**Verdict: the best risk/reward of the four. At ₩1,239,000 the stock is 58% below its June high and already prices a downturn; my base value is ₩1,322,000 (+7%), with the bull case at +77%.** This is the cleanest way to own the HBM scarcity thesis at today's prices.

**Latest results.** Q2 2026 (April to June) revenue was ₩79.3T, up 257% from a year earlier, with operating profit of ₩60.5T, a 76% margin ([StorageNewsletter](https://www.storagenewsletter.com/2026/07/30/sk-hynix-fiscal-2q26-financial-results/)). Net cash is about ₩66.9T. For Q3, management guided DRAM bit shipments up about 10% quarter over quarter. Q3 results are due October 28.

**Why the stock fell so far.** Q2 revenue and profit came in about 5% below consensus, HBM prices fixed under long-term contracts lagged the jump in commodity DRAM prices, 2026 capex was raised to the high ₩40T range (from ₩30.2T in 2025), and the company delayed its updated shareholder return plan to later in 2026 ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/us-stocks/why-sk-hynix-skhy-stock-falling-q2-earnings-analysis)). Unwinding of leveraged single-stock products amplified the selloff.

**Pricing power.** SK hynix holds about 58% of the HBM market. HBM4 mass shipments began in Q2, with the main ramp in the second half of 2026. Contract pricing caps the upside, as July showed, but also cushions the downside if commodity DRAM rolls over first.

**The cross-market outlier.** SK hynix's Q2 revenue (about $60B at ₩1,331 per dollar) was larger than Micron's latest quarter ($54.2B), yet its market value is about ₩915T (roughly $690B) against Micron's $1.03T. The gap reflects Korea's governance discount and uncertainty on capital returns more than the business.

**What the price implies.** Holding my base margins, today's price implies revenue falling about 12% a year after 2027, a real downturn. I use a 13% discount rate, one point above the US names for Korea and governance risk; at 12% the base value is ₩1,415,000.

**How to own it.** The ADR (SKHY) represents one-tenth of a share and traded at about a 22% premium to the Seoul shares in July, so compare the ADR price with the local price before buying.

**Entry range.** Below about ₩1,125,000 for a full position (base less 15%); the bull and base skew is the best of the group, but it rests on memory prices staying high through 2028. What would change the view: a weak Q4 guide on HBM4 volumes, or capex for 2027 rising well above 2026.

## Seagate (STX)

**Verdict: a great business at a full price. At $688.68 Seagate is 40% below its 52-week high, but my base value is $425 and even the bull case ($621) sits below today's price.** The stock prices hard-drive pricing power as if it were permanent, and this is the market where that is hardest to defend.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (April to June) revenue was $3.63B, up 48%, with a record 52.7% gross margin, a 44.6% operating margin and $5.71 of non-GAAP EPS. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was $3.1B and debt fell $1.4B. Guidance for fiscal Q1 2027 (July to September) is $4.1B of revenue, about a 50% operating margin and $7.30 of EPS ([Nasdaq call summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/seagate-technology-q4-earnings-call-highlights-ai-storage-demand)). Results are due October 27.

**Pricing power, the bull case.** Seagate sells on a build-to-order model. Most nearline exabytes are allocated into calendar 2028, pricing is set through calendar 2027, and customers are asking for visibility into 2029 ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/seagate-fell-8-today-why-the-market-is-ignoring-the-record-backlog)). It holds drive units flat and grows by putting more terabytes on each drive. HAMR drives were about 40% of nearline exabytes exiting fiscal 2026, roughly a year ahead of Western Digital's first HAMR product.

**Pricing power, the problem.** Hard drive capacity is cheap to add. Toshiba's plan to double output by the end of its fiscal 2027 for about $400M took the stock down 9% to 11% in early October, and the stock is now about 27% below its October 2 level. Seagate also has exchangeable notes due 2028 that could dilute shareholders ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/13/rapid-growth-high-volatility-and-near-50-upside-confound-seagate-technology-investors/)).

**On SSDs.** Seagate does not make NAND flash, and its revenue is overwhelmingly hard drives. It benefits from SSD shortages indirectly, because expensive flash pushes more cold data onto disks, but it does not capture flash pricing itself.

The bear case is not extreme by hard-drive standards. Seagate's revenue fell from $11.7B in fiscal 2022 to $7.4B in fiscal 2023, and its operating margin went from 17% to slightly negative ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stx/financials/)).

**What the price implies.** At my base margins, $689 requires revenue to compound about 28% a year from fiscal 2027 to 2031, which means exabyte growth above 25% with price per terabyte flat or rising for five years. My base fiscal 2027 EPS of about $35 is close to the $36.27 consensus, so the gap with the Street ($1,138 average target) is about the years after 2027, not next year. At a 10% discount rate the base value rises to $560, still below the price.

**Entry range.** Below about $360 (base less 15%), or sooner if fiscal Q1 margins clear 50% and long-term agreements extend set pricing into 2028 and beyond. Watch the October 27 call for what Seagate says about Toshiba and 2028 pricing.

## Western Digital (WDC)

**Verdict: wait. At $369.77 WD is 54% below its 52-week high, but my base value is $214 and the bull case is $311.** It is cheaper than Seagate on earnings but a year behind on HAMR, and it carries the same structural risk from Toshiba.

**Latest results.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (April to June) revenue was $3.75B, up 44%, with a 54.4% gross margin, a 44.2% operating margin and $3.56 of non-GAAP EPS. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $12.9B with $3.5B of free cash flow. Guidance for fiscal Q1 2027 (July to September) is $4.1B of revenue, a 55% to 56% gross margin and $4.00 of EPS ([Nasdaq call summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/western-digital-q4-earnings-call-highlights)). Results are due October 22, after the close.

**Pricing power.** WD shipped 231 exabytes in the quarter, up 22%, while cost per terabyte fell about 8%. Price per terabyte is rising, and long-term agreement talks now reach into 2029 to 2031, though pricing terms are not final. That is the key difference from Seagate: WD's long-dated volume is not yet locked at set prices.

**Technology position.** WD ships ePMR drives up to 40 TB and is ramping UltraSMR, but its first HAMR drive (44 TB) is not due until the first half of calendar 2027. If price per terabyte starts to fall, the maker with the lower cost per terabyte keeps its margin longer, which favors Seagate.

**Capital returns.** WD bought back $1B of stock in the quarter and has about $0.4B of net cash. GAAP net income has recently run well above operating income because of non-operating gains, so I value it on operating profit.

## Valuation

**SK hynix is the only name whose bull case outweighs its bear case: +77% against −47%.** For the other three, today's price sits at or above my bull case (the hard drive makers) or well above base (Micron).


**How the models work.** Each is a five-year free cash flow DCF with a terminal value, run three times. Free cash flow is EBIT after tax, less net capital spending above depreciation and the working capital that growth absorbs. The discount rate is 12% (betas above 2 against a 5.3% 10-year Treasury), 13% for SK hynix. Terminal growth is 2.5% for memory and 4% for hard drives. Net cash is added and values are per current share, so Seagate's exchangeable notes would lower its figure. This is a cash flow model, not a full three-statement build.

**Read these as a bear-leaning view.** My base cases assume the shortage eases from 2029, which is a year earlier than Micron's and SK hynix's own outlooks. Street targets are far higher (Micron $1,561, Seagate $1,138, WD $669) because they hold peak margins for longer.
