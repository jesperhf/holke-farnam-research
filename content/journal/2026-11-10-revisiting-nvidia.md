Nvidia is about 3% of my portfolio at an average cost of $126.94. When I built its model on October 23 the stock was $212.28, and the base case earned 9.3% a year, below the 11% I required, so it was a hold with no adds. The stock has since fallen to about $171, and nothing in the business has changed since August's results. This entry revisits the valuation at the new price.

## Summary

**Nvidia has moved from expensive to fair, not to cheap.** My base value is $175 a share. At $171.00, the base case earns about 10.8% a year against the 10.6% I require, close to the line.

| Price | $212.28 | $171.00 |
| Base value | $165 | $175 |
| Required return | 11.0% | 10.6% |
| Base-case return at the price | 9.3% a year | 10.8% a year |

Today's price is from 10:40 am ET, November 10 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-drops-3-extending-decline-on-report-alleged-chinese-spy-linked-to-nvidia-ceo-aided-75-billion-in-china-chip-shipments-152654028.html)). The base value rose from $165 to $175 only because the 10-year Treasury yield fell from 5.31% to 4.89% between the two dates; the business assumptions are unchanged.

- **The price now assumes about my base case.** That case is already below consensus: fiscal 2028 revenue of $640 billion against the roughly 70% growth Nvidia guided, and operating margin easing from 62% to 46% over the long run as custom chips compete.
- **The range around it is wide.** The bear value is $99, 42% below the price, and the bull value $250, 46% above it.
- **Fair is not a reason to buy.** I am looking for undervalued names. At this price Nvidia offers no margin of safety, so it stays a hold.

I would add below about $150, 15% under the base value, and not before the November 17 report.

## What the price implies

At $171 the stock trades between my bear and base values, almost on the base. The price ladder below shows the annual return the base case earns at each price.

**On earnings.** My base case has fiscal 2028 after-tax operating earnings of about $13.80 a share, which puts the stock at about 12 times. On consensus revenue of $692 billion and a 64% operating margin, it would be about 11 times. That is a low multiple for a company growing this fast, and it is low because the market is now pricing a fall in margins after fiscal 2028, which my model assumes too.

**One rate caveat.** October's inflation report came in hot this morning, and the 10-year yield traded near 4.99% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-drops-3-extending-decline-on-report-alleged-chinese-spy-linked-to-nvidia-ceo-aided-75-billion-in-china-chip-shipments-152654028.html)). If that holds into Wednesday's weekly rate update, my base value falls about 1.4%, to about $173. That does not change the conclusion.

## Checking the base case against the latest numbers

**Growth is ahead of my base case.** Revenue in the July quarter was $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier, with data center revenue of $89.0 billion. Nvidia guided the October quarter to $108 billion, with no data center compute revenue from China ([Q2 release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/)). Management said it expects about 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028 and called the outlook supply constrained ([Zacks summary of the call](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2981024/nvidia-q2-earnings-call-puts-supply-limits-at-center-of-outlook)). My base case has 55%. I am keeping it below guidance on purpose: the late-cycle risk in my [AI cycle entry](the-ai-trade-and-the-business-cycle.html) is that this growth is being financed in ways that may not last.

**Demand from the largest buyer group is still rising.** Alphabet said on October 28 that it continues to expect capex to "increase significantly in 2027" ([Alphabet Q3 call](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_events/2026/Oct/28/2026_Q3_Earnings_Transcript.pdf)). No hyperscaler has guided 2027 capex flat or down, which is one of my two thesis-break signals.

**Gross margin is moving toward my other signal.** Nvidia guided October-quarter gross margin to 74% ([Q2 release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/)), and said on the call it expects a low of 71% to 72% in the January quarter on memory costs before recovering to 72% to 73% ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2981024/nvidia-q2-earnings-call-puts-supply-limits-at-center-of-outlook)). My sell line is 70%. My model already has operating margin falling from 62% to 46%, so a lower gross margin is in the base case; a guide below 70% would still trip the signal.

**Cash flow is lagging earnings.** The July quarter shows a gap between profit and cash that my model only partly captures.

Sources: [Q2 release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/) and [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm). The April-quarter cash flow is the six-month figure of $74.4 billion less the July quarter.

The 10-Q attributes the receivables increase to "extended payment terms on large multi-quarter agreements." Receivables now equal about 60 days of revenue. Up to $105 billion of the guarantees cover data center leases at a single campus in Ohio, and OpenAI has agreed to reimburse certain losses under them. This is Nvidia financing its own demand: longer payment terms, stakes in customers and guarantees of their leases. My model leaves the $99 billion of equity stakes out of value and assumes working capital absorbs 10% of revenue growth. The July quarter ran well above that. If cash conversion does not improve, the base value is too high, not too low.

**Competition is in the model, not new to it.** Google's eighth-generation TPUs, split into a training chip and an inference chip, become generally available this year, with up to twice the performance per watt of the previous generation ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/eighth-generation-tpu-agentic-era/)). Google added two more TPU systems on November 9 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-monday-november-9-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-090535419.html)). Broadcom, which designs custom accelerators including Google's TPUs, guided AI semiconductor revenue to $21.7 billion for its October quarter, up 236% from a year earlier ([Broadcom 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001730168/000173016826000076/avgo-08022026x8kxex99.htm)). That is about a quarter of Nvidia's quarterly data center revenue, and growing faster. Custom chips taking share is why my base case has margins falling well below today's.

## The leadership question

On November 8, Nvidia's board said a committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate reports that Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised and that it has confidence in Huang ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). The underlying reports are about a Singapore customer and whether Nvidia chips reached China ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-drops-3-extending-decline-on-report-alleged-chinese-spy-linked-to-nvidia-ceo-aided-75-billion-in-china-chip-shipments-152654028.html)). None of it has been established.

I do not change the cash flows for this. As a rough size: if there were a 10% to 25% chance Huang left, and his leaving moved the business halfway from my base case to my bear case, the expected cost would be about $4 to $9 a share, 2% to 5% of the base value. The larger business risk is export rules: a finding that chips were diverted through Southeast Asia could tighten sales into the region. Nvidia's guidance already excludes China data center compute, so the exposure is to the rest of Asia.

## What would make it undervalued

- **Price.** Below about $150, the base case earns 11.8% a year, and the stock sits 15% under my base value. That cushion would cover the cash flow and leadership risks without needing the bull case.
- **The November 17 report.** Revenue at or above the $108 billion guided, gross margin near 74% with no cut to the January-quarter outlook, and operating cash flow moving back toward net income. A strong report would lift my confidence in the base case, not the value itself, since growth is already above my assumptions.
- **Cash conversion.** Receivables and commitments growing slower than revenue for two quarters would remove the biggest discount I would otherwise apply.

## What I am doing

- **Hold.** Neither thesis-break signal has fired: no hyperscaler has guided 2027 capex flat or down, and gross margin is guided above 70%.
- **No adds at fair value.** I add when a stock is undervalued, and at $171 Nvidia is not.
- **Add below about $150**, after the November 17 report, if the report holds up.
- **Trim above $250**, the bull value, and review below $135, halfway between bear and base.
