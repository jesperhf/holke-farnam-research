I have a lot of cash after the [ServiceNow trim](../positions.html), and the [cycle tracker](../tracker.html) sits right between mid and late cycle. Late in a cycle, the sectors that have tended to lead are healthcare, energy, materials and consumer staples. The portfolio already holds a large staples position, so this entry looks at whether healthcare deserves more of the cash, and which names. I looked at energy at the same time; it is not part of this entry.

## Summary

**Healthcare is the sector to own into a recession as well as before one, and the value left in it is in medical devices.** The sector's leaders have rerated: Johnson & Johnson is up 41% over the year and Merck 66%. Devices have not: Abbott is down 19% over the year and Medtronic is flat, both at 16x to 18x forward earnings.

- **Three of the four are worth buying now.** Abbott, Medtronic and Becton Dickinson have base values 17% to 27% above their prices, and their bear cases sit only 9% to 15% below the price.
- **Merck comes off the list for now.** Its price already needs something close to the bull case, in which newer drugs replace most of Keytruda after 2028.
- **The plan:** take the healthcare sleeve from 9.8% of the portfolio to about 15%, with about 1.7% in each of the three device makers. Half now, half after each company's next results.

## Where the cycle stands, and why healthcare

The cycle tracker read 50 out of 100 on October 26, exactly between mid and late cycle, with 6 of 11 indicators voting mid and 5 voting late. The late votes come from inflation and policy: CPI at 3.7% and rising, the Fed raising its target to 4.00%, a 2.84% real 10-year yield, payroll growth down to 51k a month, and Brent up 94% over the year. The mid votes come from the real economy: unemployment at 4.2%, jobless claims at 198k, GDP growth of 2.2% and profits up 26.6%. That mix is the classic late-cycle setup, an inflation shock meeting an economy that has not cracked yet.

Fidelity's business cycle work says defensive and inflation-resistant sectors tend to lead late in the cycle. Consumer staples and utilities have usually outperformed in both the late and recession phases ([Fidelity](https://institutional.fidelity.com/app/item/RD_13569_21124/sector-investing.html)). Healthcare belongs in the same defensive group, because demand for care does not depend much on the economy. That is the point of it here: a sector to own into a recession, not just until one starts.

Healthcare has gone from a discount of up to 25% to the market a year ago to a small premium ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e6ee1dfa-c0e7-4a11-813b-cba85e368c14)). It is not cheap the way it would be at the start of a rotation; much of the rerating has happened.

**What the portfolio already holds.** Healthcare is 9.8% of the portfolio: UnitedHealth at 6.4% and the biotech ETF at 3.3%. Staples are 17.1% (Altria 8.7%, Coca-Cola 8.4%). Cash was 17.2% after the ServiceNow trim.

## The healthcare map

The easy part of the rerating is done. The value left is in the parts that did not rally, mainly medical devices and, to a lesser degree, managed care.

**Policy is calmer than a year ago, not gone.** More than ten drug makers have signed deals with the administration, trading price concessions for tariff exemptions ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e6ee1dfa-c0e7-4a11-813b-cba85e368c14)). AllianceBernstein counts 16 such deals and puts the planned Medicaid cuts at about $1 trillion over a decade, under 2% of annual US health spending ([AllianceBernstein](https://www.alliancebernstein.com/corporate/en/insights/investment-insights/are-healthcare-stocks-poised-to-recover-as-policy-fears-stabilize.html)). A divided Congress after the November 3 midterms would make new legislation less likely. The live risk is for insurers, if Medicare for All comes back into the 2028 primaries.

**Managed care is past its worst year.** CMS finalized a 2.48% Medicare Advantage rate increase for 2027, against 0.09% in its January proposal ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/04/07/5-stocks-that-should-benefit-from-the-2026-medicare-advantage-rate-decision/)). Elevance raised 2026 guidance to at least $29 a share and guided 2027 to at least 12% growth from a $26.75 base, calling 2026 the Medicaid trough ([Elevance Q3 call](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-elevance-health-tops-q3-2026-estimates-raises-outlook-93CH-5645743)). About $1.85 of its Q3 beat came from investment gains, so the underlying improvement is smaller than the headline. Humana jumped 15% when its 2027 star ratings improved ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/09/humana-soars-16-on-improved-medicare-advantage-star-ratings-cvs-slides-3-unitedhealth-holds-steady/)).

**Medical devices are where the discount is.** Abbott fell after a Q4 revenue miss in January, an FDA-linked recall of some FreeStyle Libre 3 sensors, and weak nutrition sales, while it absorbs the Exact Sciences cancer screening deal ([Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/284326/why-abbott-laboratories-abt-shares-are-sliding-today), [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/abbott-laboratories-stock-is-down-16-from-its-52-week-high-heres-where-the-stock-could-go-in-2026)). Guidance still calls for 2026 EPS of $5.68, about 18x at the price. Medtronic has gone nowhere for a year and yields 3.0%.

**Merck is cheaper than its trailing numbers look.** 2026 EPS of about $2.97 is depressed by acquisition charges, while 2027 consensus is $9.66, about 15x the price ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2980276/merck-stock-hits-new-52-week-high:-what's-driving-the-rally)). The question is how much of Keytruda's revenue, $16.4 billion in the first half, Winrevair and the newer launches can replace after 2028.

## Valuation results

**The device makers have limited downside, which is what a late-cycle buy should look like.** The models are ten-year DCFs on the same framework and discount-rate inputs as my holdings models: five explicit years, a five-year fade to terminal levels, and adjusted operating margins after stock pay. Discount rates are an assumed 8.0% for all four; the CAPM figure on the WACC tab is lower for each, because their betas are so low.

- **Abbott.** The base case assumes about 6.5% revenue growth and margins edging from 24.5% to 26%. The bear case has continuous glucose monitor pricing pressure and Libre recall costs taking share.
- **Medtronic.** The base case follows consensus to fiscal 2028 and then grows about 4.5%. The bull case needs pulsed field ablation and renal denervation to ramp.
- **Becton Dickinson.** The base case assumes about 4% growth and margins moving toward 26% after the Waters separation.

None of the three needs a margin story it has not already shown.

**Merck is priced close to its bull case of $138.** The base case has revenue falling about 11% from 2027 to 2030 as Keytruda loses exclusivity, then recovering. The price assumes the newer drugs replace most of it. A price under about $100, or launch data that make the bull case look likely, would put it back on the list.

The workbook, with every scenario path and the discount-rate switch, is on the [models page](../models.html).

## Sizing and pacing

| | Now | Proposed | How |

| Cash | 17.2% | Lower, with a reserve kept | T-bills or a money market fund while it waits |

These sizes are a starting point. Final sizes should follow each model's base value and return at the price, the same way the positions page works. With the Fed at 4.00% and still raising, cash earns a real return while it waits, and a late-cycle portfolio should keep dry powder for the contraction my [AI trade research](the-ai-trade-in-a-late-cycle.html) worries about. The high-yield fund is the holding most at odds with a late-cycle view, and selling it would add about 4 points to the cash available.

## What would change the view

- **The tracker moving.** A reading above 62 would mean late cycle in full: speed up the healthcare adds. Defensive rotations that start early can lag for a long time, though: rotating into defensives early in 2006 led to nearly two years of underperformance ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e6ee1dfa-c0e7-4a11-813b-cba85e368c14)).
- **Policy.** A wider drug-pricing order, a revived Medicare for All push or pharmacy benefit reform would hit pharma or insurers. The November 3 midterms are the next marker.
- **Company risks.** Abbott's Libre recall and the Exact Sciences integration, hospital capital budgets for Medtronic and Becton Dickinson in a downturn, and Merck's 2028 Keytruda patent expiry.
- **The next reports.** Merck and Becton Dickinson report on October 29. The second half of each device position waits for that company's next results.
