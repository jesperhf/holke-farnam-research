CoreWeave's third-quarter report on November 10 drove most of today's rally in AI stocks. Its shares rose about 25%, and Nvidia, Nebius and the power and optical suppliers followed. My [October sector work](the-ai-trade-and-the-business-cycle.html) rated neoclouds lowest on a risk-adjusted basis because the leverage in the AI buildout sits there. This entry tests whether the quarter changes that, using CoreWeave's own call and filings and a valuation model on the same framework as my holdings.

## Summary

**The quarter improved the business and its funding. It did not make the stock cheap.**

| | Value per share at a 10% cost of capital |
| --- | --- |
| Price, November 11, 1:04 pm ET | $61.18 |
| Bear: capacity glut after 2027, renewals reprice down | below zero |
| Base: backlog converts, renewals at today's prices, capex slows after 2027 | about −$12, so roughly zero |
| Bull: capacity stays sold out, renewals reprice up | $90 |

Price: Nasdaq data via [Stock Titan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-11-2026), up 25.5% on the day from a $48.73 close. A value below zero means the debt is worth more than the business in that case; the equity is then an option on a better outcome.

- **The operating story is strong.** Revenue grew 168% to $3.66 billion, backlog reached about $133 billion, and management says capacity is largely sold out through 2027 at rising prices.
- **The funding story improved.** The average cost of debt fell to 7.9%, about $9 billion of customer prepayments are due in the next three months, and management plans no new unsecured debt at the parent through 2027.
- **The equity is a thin layer on top of the debt.** CoreWeave carries $46.9 billion of debt against a market value of about $34 billion. Small changes in long-run margin or cost of capital swing the equity from zero to above today's price.
- **The price needs close to the bull case.** At $61, the market is either discounting my base case at below 8% or expecting margins near 28% and steady growth into the 2030s.

I am passing. This is not an undervalued stock; it is a leveraged bet on the bull case, which my late-cycle view says to avoid.

## What the quarter said

All figures are from CoreWeave's [Q3 2026 earnings call](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-coreweave-tops-q3-2026-forecast-and-shares-rise-57-93CH-5017354) unless noted.

| | Q3 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | $3.66B, up 168% |
| Backlog | About $133B, up 138% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $2.3B, 62% of revenue |
| Adjusted operating income | $379M, 10% of revenue (5% in Q2, 1% in Q1) |
| Interest expense | $846M |
| Net loss | $709M |
| Operating cash flow | $2.0B |
| Capex (company measure) | $14.5B |

| 2026 outlook | Now |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | $13.0B to $13.3B, raised |
| Adjusted operating income | $1.275B to $1.375B |
| Capex | $37B to $39B, low end raised from $35B |
| Active power at year-end | More than 1.85 GW |
| Annualized revenue run rate at exit | $20B to $21B |

**Pricing is rising.** Short-term capacity is selling for more than $40 million per megawatt a year, and four- to five-year contracts at $20 to $25 million. Older Ampere and Hopper GPUs repriced up 30% to 50% from the prior quarter. More than 130,000 GPUs come off contract in 2027 and about 170,000 in 2028; management estimates renewing them at today's prices adds about $4 billion of annual revenue in 2027 and nearly $10 billion including 2028, with no new capex.

**Funding is cheaper and more of it comes from customers.** The weighted average cost of debt is 7.9%, down 2.5 points from a year ago. About $9 billion of prepayments and deposits are due over the next three months, and prepayments were collected on 70% of this year's deals. Management will not issue unsecured debt at the parent at current yields and expects less than $5 billion of new parent-level capital through 2027. The next delayed-draw term loan is expected to be investment grade, financing more than 90% of its capex.

**2027.** Management said capex could stay roughly in line with 2026 if it only builds for committed backlog, and that adjusted EBITDA less interest should more than double.

**Interest still exceeds operating profit.** Adjusted operating income was $379 million against $846 million of interest, and Q4 interest is guided to $1.05 to $1.15 billion. Earnings before interest cover the interest bill only on an EBITDA basis, which ignores the depreciation of GPUs that must be replaced.

## The valuation

The model is a ten-year DCF on the same framework as my holdings: five explicit years, a five-year fade to terminal levels, operating margins after depreciation and stock pay, and reinvestment equal to capex less depreciation. The equity value is the business value less net debt of $40.4 billion. The discount rate is 10%: CoreWeave's equity costs about 15% at a beta near 2, and its debt about 7.9% before tax, weighted at roughly 40% equity and 60% debt at market values.

**Why margins drive everything.** Depreciation is about half of revenue today, because capacity is installed and depreciated before it earns. At management's long-contract pricing of $20 to $25 million per megawatt a year, and my estimate of $30 to $35 million of capex per megawatt depreciated over six years, a mature operating margin after stock pay lands in the low to high 20s. My base case reaches 24% by 2031 and fades to 22%. The bear case stays near 10%, and the bull case reaches 30%.

| Base case | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $22B | $30B | $37B | $42B | $46B |
| Operating margin | 12% | 17% | 21% | 23% | 24% |
| Free cash flow | −$21.7B | −$6.7B | −$1.7B | $1.6B | $2.9B |

The base case does not turn free cash flow positive until 2030. Everything before then is funded with debt and prepayments.

**Base value per share, by long-run margin and cost of capital:**

| Terminal operating margin | 8% | 9% | 10% | 11% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 22% (base) | $45 | $12 | −$12 | −$29 |
| 25% | $81 | $41 | $13 | −$8 |
| 28% | $118 | $72 | $39 | $15 |

Other changes matter much less: 10% more revenue every year adds about $6 a share, and cutting 2027 net capex by about a fifth adds about $7.

**What the price implies.** At $61, the stock needs a margin near 28% with a cost of capital a little above 9%, or my base margin with a cost of capital below 8%. At 10%, it needs about a 70% chance of my bull case. That is the market treating CoreWeave as a utility with growth. Its customers sign multi-year contracts, but its assets are GPUs with short economic lives, and its top three customers are 72% of revenue.

## What this means for the late-cycle view

The quarter weakens one part of my October view and leaves the core intact. Demand and pricing are stronger than I expected, and moving funding toward customer prepayments and investment-grade loans lowers the refinancing risk I flagged. But the equity still sits on $46.9 billion of debt, interest exceeds operating profit, and free cash flow stays negative until 2030 in my base case. Late in a cycle, with the Fed expected to raise rates in December, that structure is where losses concentrate if AI capex slows. Today's 25% move shows how much of the AI trade's sentiment now runs through this one company.

For my own holdings, the read-through is positive for [Nvidia](revisiting-nvidia.html), whose GPUs CoreWeave rents out. It is also another example of a supplier's demand being financed by its customers' debt.

## What I am doing

- **Pass.** CoreWeave is not undervalued on my numbers, and I am not adding leverage-exposed AI names this late in the cycle.
- **What would change it:** operating margin, not EBITDA margin, moving toward 20% while interest stays covered by operating income; or a price low enough that my base case at 10% is worth more than the price, which today it is not.
- **What I will watch:** the Q4 report for debt growth against revenue growth, renewal pricing on the GPUs coming off contract in 2027, and customer concentration in the 10-K.
