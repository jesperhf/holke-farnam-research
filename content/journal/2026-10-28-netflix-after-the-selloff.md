Netflix is one of two tech names that have been beaten down recently that I think could turn; [Uber](uber-after-the-selloff.html) is the other. This entry values Netflix on the same framework and discount-rate inputs as the models behind my [positions page](../positions.html).

## Summary

**Netflix is fairly priced, not cheap.** My base value is about $66 a share against a price of $62.52. The base case earns 9.8% a year at that price, just above the 9.5% I require.

| | Price | Discount rate | Bear | Base | Bull | Base-case return at the price | Buy below |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Netflix | $62.52 | 9.5% | $39 | $66 | $91 | 9.8% a year | $55 |

Price: Stock Analysis, October 27 close. Values are per share; margins are after stock pay. Buy below here is about 15% under the base value, not the base value itself, for the reason below.

- **The selloff took it from expensive to fair.** At about 17 times next year's earnings, against a three-year average near 33 times, the base case earns about the required return.
- **There is no margin of safety yet.** The bear case is 37% below the price, and it only needs growth to slow to about 4% a year and margins to stop rising.
- **It is the more defensive of my two beaten-down tech names.** A streaming subscription survives a recession better than discretionary rides and delivery, though Netflix's beta of 1.61 is still high.

**Verdict.** Netflix is just under its $66 base value, but with the bear case 37% lower I would wait for about $55, 15% under the base value, before owning a real position.

## Why it sold off

**The stock is near its 52-week low on slower growth today.** It closed October 27 at $62.52, against a 52-week range of $62.48 to $116.73, down 42% over the year, at 17.4 times forward earnings against a three-year average of about 33 times ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nflx/), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/netflix-stock-down-41-much-193322821.html)).

The slide started last fall. Netflix agreed in December to buy Warner Bros. Discovery's studio and streaming assets, then walked away on February 26 when a rival bid won, collecting a $2.8 billion termination fee ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/netflix-stock-down-41-much-193322821.html)). The October 20 report then cut the 2026 ad revenue outlook to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion, nudged revenue guidance to $51.1 billion, below the prior midpoint, and lowered free cash flow to about $12 billion from $12.5 billion. Management also acknowledged investor concern about viewing-hours growth ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065280/000106528026000290/ex991_q326.htm), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/netflix-ad-revenue-forecast-drops-to-2-7b-as-partner-integrations-slip/)). Consensus has 2027 EPS growing only about 6% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/netflix-stock-down-41-much-193322821.html)).

## The quarter

**Profits are still growing fast; revenue is what slowed.** Q3 revenue grew 12% to $12.9 billion, in line with the forecast, while operating income rose 37% and the margin reached 34.6%, helped by the timing of expenses ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065280/000106528026000290/ex991_q326.htm)).

| Operating margin | 34.6% (28.2% a year ago) | 31.5% (29.5% in 2025) |
| Free cash flow | $2.9B | About $12.0B (from $12.5B) |

Source: [Netflix Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065280/000106528026000290/ex991_q326.htm). Q4 guidance is $13.4 billion of revenue at a 26.0% margin, including about 1.2 points of cost from recent headcount cuts. Churn improved year over year, and the ad tier launches in 15 more countries in 2027.

## Scenarios and value

| Scenario | Revenue 2031 | Operating margin 2031 / long run | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear: viewing keeps sliding to YouTube and AI video | $63B | 30% / 27% | $39 |
| Base: growth slows to about 7%, margins edge up | $74B | 35.5% / 33% | $66 |
| Bull: ads and pricing compound, margins near 39% | $82B | 39% / 37% | $91 |

**How the model treats Netflix.** Content is the reinvestment: cash content spend runs about 1.1 times amortization, so reinvestment is set at 4% of revenue, including capex. That reproduces the guided $12 billion of 2026 free cash flow. Net debt is $7.1 billion. The discount rate is 9.5%, the same as Alphabet's in my holdings models; CAPM gives 12.0% because Netflix's five-year beta is 1.61.

**What the price implies.** At $62.52 the stock trades almost exactly at the base value, so it earns about the required return if growth settles near 7% and margins keep creeping up. The base case is a little below consensus in the near term: 2027 revenue of $55.5 billion, up about 9%, and about $3.61 a share of after-tax operating earnings against $3.77 of consensus EPS. The bear case is not far-fetched, since it only needs growth to slow to about 4% a year and margins to stop rising.

## What would change the view

| | Would make me buy more confidently | Would make me walk away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Netflix | View hours growing again; ad revenue back on its $3 billion path in 2027; revenue growth holding near 10% | Revenue growth below 8% for two quarters; another price increase that does not stick; margin expansion stopping |

**Late-cycle fit.** Netflix is not a late-cycle defensive, but it is a better recession holding than most of tech: a streaming subscription is among the last things households cut, and the company buys back stock with $12 billion of free cash flow a year.

**Sizing, if bought.** A growth bet alongside the [healthcare additions](healthcare-in-a-late-cycle.html), not instead of them, at 1% to 1.5% below about $55, where a 15% cushion covers part of the gap to the bear case.

**Model notes.** Ten-year DCF fading to terminal levels, margins after stock pay, a 5.31% risk-free rate and a 5% equity premium. The weekly Treasury update covers the holdings models only, so this one gets rerun by hand if the rate moves before a decision.
