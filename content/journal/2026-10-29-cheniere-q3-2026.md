Cheniere is 5.6% of my portfolio at an average cost of $192.69, and with Enterprise Products it is the fee-based energy exposure my [healthcare entry](healthcare-in-a-late-cycle.html) said the portfolio already has. It reported its third quarter before the open on October 29. This is the update to the valuation behind my [positions page](../positions.html).

## Summary

**The quarter raised the value, not just the earnings.** Cheniere raised its 2026 guidance for the second quarter in a row, and the balance sheet and share count both improved. My base value rises from $330 to $380 a share. At yesterday's close of $297.77, the base case now earns about 8.2% a year against the 7.2% I require.

The "before" column already includes yesterday's lower Treasury rate, which took the base value from $305 to $330. All of the move from $330 to $380 comes from the results.

- **Guidance went up again.** 2026 adjusted EBITDA is now $8.75 to $9.25 billion, from $7.9 to $8.4 billion, and distributable cash flow is $6.05 to $6.55 billion, from $5.3 to $5.8 billion.
- **Contracting has not stalled.** Cheniere signed up to 4 mtpa with QatarEnergy through 2054 and 0.8 mtpa with Petrobras for 22 years. That is the opposite of the thesis-break signal on my positions page.
- **The stock barely reacted.** It was up about 2% before the open, so the gap between price and value widened.

I am holding. The exit level rises to $280, about 6% below yesterday's close, which is the one thing in this update that needs watching.

**Capital returns.** Cheniere bought back about 2.8 million shares for $731 million in the quarter, $1.8 billion so far this year, and raised the quarterly dividend more than 12% to $0.625.

**Projects.** Train 7 at Corpus Christi Stage 3 reached substantial completion in August, finishing the seven-train project ahead of schedule and within budget and adding over 20% to production capacity. Trains 8 and 9 are 58.6% complete and due in the second half of 2028. The Sabine Pass expansion has its final environmental review and a lump-sum construction contract, but no investment decision yet. S&P raised the Corpus Christi credit rating to A−.

## Why the value moved

**1. A higher starting point.** Nine-month revenue was $17.77 billion, so 2026 lands near $24.3 billion against the $22.6 billion my model used. I did not carry all of it forward. Part of this year's strength is wartime spot margins, and the EIA expects Henry Hub gas to fall about 9% in 2027. My base case holds 2027 revenue near this year's level at $24.6 billion, then grows with Stage 3 at full rate and trains 8 and 9 in 2028. The margin stays at 31%, as before.

**2. Less debt.** Net debt fell to about $22.3 billion from $26.9 billion in my last model. Spread over the share count, that adds about $22 a share on its own.

**3. Fewer shares.** Buybacks took the count to about 205 million from 207 million.

**The bear case improved most in percentage terms**, up 21%, because it starts from a higher base year and carries the same lower debt. That is also why the exit level jumps to $280: it sits halfway between bear and base, and both moved up.

61% of the base value comes from years after 2036, so the long-term contract book matters more than this year's spot margins. The QatarEnergy and Petrobras deals add to the contracted base that I care about most.

## What it means for the late-cycle view

Cheniere is the part of energy I wanted to own late in the cycle: mostly sold under long-term contracts and paid on volume more than on the oil price. This quarter got some help from the conflict through spot margins, and that will fade. The contracts, the finished expansion and the A− rating will not. The risk in my bear case is still the 2027 to 2028 global LNG supply wave weakening spot margins and new contracting, and this quarter's two long-term deals argue against it for now.

## What I am doing

- **Hold.** The thesis-break signals (a cut to cash flow guidance, or stalled contracting) moved the other way.
- **No add today.** The base case now clears my required return, so adding is allowed by my own rule. Energy is already about 10% of the portfolio through Cheniere and Enterprise Products, so I am not adding for now.
- **Trim a third above $530**, the new bull value, up from $475.
- **Review below $280**, the new exit level. A normal pullback could reach it, so it is a reason to reread the thesis, not an automatic sale.
