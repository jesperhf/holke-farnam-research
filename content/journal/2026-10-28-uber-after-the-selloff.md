Uber is one of two tech names that have been beaten down recently that I think could turn; [Netflix](netflix-after-the-selloff.html) is the other. This entry values Uber on the same framework and discount-rate inputs as the models behind my [positions page](../positions.html).

## Summary

**Uber looks cheap if robotaxis do not cut it out of the trip.** My base value is about $86 a share against a price of $65.84, and the base case earns about 12% a year at that price against the 10% I require.

| | Price | Discount rate | Bear | Base | Bull | Base-case return at the price | Buy below |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uber | $65.84 | 10.0% | $32 | $86 | $140 | 11.9% a year | $86 |

Price: Stock Analysis, October 27 at 11:20 ET. Values are per share; margins are after stock pay.

- **The price assumes a real chance of the bear case.** At $66, the market is pricing roughly a 37% chance that robotaxi owners keep the customer and Uber's margins shrink. If they do not, the base case earns about 12% a year.
- **The range is wide.** The bear case is 52% below the price and the bull case is more than double it.
- **It does not fit the late-cycle tilt.** Uber's beta is 1.26, and rides and delivery orders are discretionary spending that falls in a recession.

**Verdict.** Uber goes on the watchlist as a candidate to buy, sized small, at 1% to 1.5% of the portfolio. Its Q3 report on November 4 comes first and is worth waiting for.

## Why it sold off

**The business keeps growing; the fear is about who owns the customer once cars drive themselves.** The stock closed October 27 near the bottom of its 52-week range of $65.41 to $100.35, down 30% over the year, at 16.3 times forward earnings ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/uber/)).

Waymo pulled its cars from the Uber app in Phoenix in June ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/ubers-waymo-detour-tests-the-stocks-robotaxi-bull-case/)). In July the Financial Times reported Waymo plans to run Austin and Atlanta on its own from January 2028, when the current agreement allows ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/uber-stock-slides-nearly-5-205324793.html)). Uber has reportedly spent more than $10 billion over the past year on autonomous-vehicle stakes and fleet deals to secure supply. Its Q3 guidance also came in slightly below consensus: $58.25 to $60.25 billion of bookings against $59.33 billion expected, and $0.84 to $0.88 of adjusted EPS against $0.89 ([I amsterdam Startup Map](https://startupmap.iamsterdam.com/news/feed/uber-q3-guidance-misses-estimates-despite-58b-q2-bookings-and-record-10b-free-cash-flow)).

## The operating business

**The operating business is accelerating while the stock falls.** In Q2, gross bookings grew 24% to $58.0 billion, trips 18% to 3.9 billion, and GAAP operating income 30% to $1.9 billion. Trailing free cash flow passed $10 billion for the first time ([Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001543151/000154315126000027/uberq226earningspressrelea.htm)).

## The robotaxi question decides the value

Uber's answer is to be the demand layer for every autonomous fleet: a 35,000-car Nuro and Lucid fleet exclusive to Uber launches in the San Francisco Bay Area in late 2026, with WeRide abroad and other partners ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/ubers-waymo-detour-tests-the-stocks-robotaxi-bull-case/)). The risk is the Waymo pattern spreading: fleet owners that can fill their cars directly do not need Uber's cut.

| Scenario | Revenue 2031 | Operating margin 2031 / long run | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear: robotaxi owners keep the customer, take rates fall | $71B | 12% / 10% | $32 |
| Base: growth slows from 12% to 7%, margins reach about 18% | $93B | 18.5% / 18% | $86 |
| Bull: Uber becomes the demand layer for robotaxis | $106B | 23% / 23% | $140 |

**How the model treats Uber.** Margins are GAAP operating margins, so stock pay counts as a cost, and the model's free cash flow runs below Uber's reported $10 billion. Working capital is a source of cash, because Uber collects from riders before it pays drivers. Uber's equity stakes (Didi, Aurora and others) are left out, which is conservative. The discount rate is 10%; CAPM gives 10.6%.

**What the price implies.** At $65.84 the stock sits between the bear and base values. Weighting just those two, the market is pricing about a 37% chance of the bear case. At a 9% discount rate the base value would be $103; at 11%, $74.

## What would change the view

| | Would make me buy more confidently | Would make me walk away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uber | Mobility bookings holding above 20% growth in Waymo's cities; more exclusive fleet deals like Nuro and Lucid; stock pay falling as a share of revenue | Another major autonomous fleet leaving the app; take rates falling as fleets negotiate; margins stalling below 15% |

**Next test: Q3 results, expected November 4** ([Bullstory](https://bullstory.io/en/stocks/uber/earnings/2026-q3)). What matters is whether Mobility bookings keep growing above 20% in Waymo's cities, and any update on autonomous supply deals.

**Sizing, if bought.** This would be a growth bet alongside the [healthcare additions](healthcare-in-a-late-cycle.html), not instead of them. It should start small, 1% to 1.5%, because the outcome range runs from half the price to double it.

**Model notes.** Ten-year DCF fading to terminal levels, margins after stock pay, a 5.31% risk-free rate and a 5% equity premium. The weekly Treasury update covers the holdings models only, so this one gets rerun by hand if the rate moves before a decision.
